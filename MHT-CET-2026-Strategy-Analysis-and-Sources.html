1   How the Rank/Percentile Projection Was Derived (Task 1)

Logic

Percentile is a relative rank: a 94.11 percentile means she scored higher than 94.11% of the PCM cohort, so about 5.89% sit above her. Multiplying that fraction by the number of candidates who appeared for the PCM group gives an approximate State Merit Rank.

Rank  =  (100 - percentile)/100  x  PCM candidates appeared
      =  0.0589  x  N

N = 3.0 lakh  ->  ~17,700
N = 3.5 lakh  ->  ~20,600
N = 4.0 lakh  ->  ~23,600

Why a range, not a number

  • The exact PCM appeared-count for 2026 is unknown until the CET Cell publishes it; recent years have ranged ~3.0–4.0 lakh, so the output is a band (~18k–24k).
  • Category & Ladies ranks are separate, smaller queues. Inside the OBC column she is compared only with OBC candidates (~1/3 of the pool), and the 30% Ladies reservation is horizontal (applied within each category), creating an even smaller LOBC pool. This is why her effective reach is much better than the raw ~20k overall rank suggests.
Conclusion drawn: plan around an overall rank of ~23k (conservative), but target seats using the far stronger LOBC column, which historically closes ~2–5 percentile below the open cutoff for the same branch.

This table is why the buckets exist: TFWS is the only clean route into premium private colleges within budget; government/aided fits via the 50% concession; and many famous private names are eliminated unless TFWS is won.

Why TFWS was rated "hard, not easy"

  • TFWS is a very small quota (about 5% of branch intake) reserved for income-eligible (<₹8L) meritorious students.
  • Scarce seats + high-scoring applicants → TFWS cutoffs are frequently higher than open-category cutoffs for the same branch.
  • Hence the verdict: TFWS at top-5 colleges (PICT/VIT/DJS) for ENTC/IT is unlikely at 94.11, but realistic at Cummins/PCCOE.

Why MKSSS Cummins is the standout target

It is a women-only institute, so the Ladies-reservation effect is maximised (the competing pool is entirely female), making its ENTC/IT cutoffs more reachable for a strong female OBC candidate than at co-ed colleges of similar reputation — while retaining strong industry placement ties.

Why COEP/VJTI were marked "reach"

Their overall cutoffs are among the state's highest; even non-CS/core branches typically close well above 94.11 for OBC. They are therefore listed as aspirational top-of-form options, not safety nets — with the honest note that VJTI core (Textile/Production) is more reachable than anything at COEP.

3   How the Branch & "AI-Proof" Strategy Was Reasoned (Task 3 + follow-up)

The core thesis

Generative AI automates repeatable tasks (boilerplate code, basic QA, L1 support), not ownership (architecture, hardware grounding, domain judgement). So the Indian market is becoming a barbell: the routine IT-services middle shrinks, while deep-engineering specialists and AI-augmented domain workers grow.

Why ENTC was chosen as the top bet

  • It is the gateway to VLSI / semiconductors and embedded / EV / IoT — the strongest, most policy-backed Indian tailwinds and the most AI-resistant (hardware can't be "prompted" into existence).
  • It can still pivot to software/data, so it keeps both doors open.
  • Its LOBC cutoffs are reachable at good Pune colleges at 94.11, unlike the overcrowded CS column.

Why "aim at GCCs" is the central career instruction

Global Capability Centers (captive R&D/product arms of MNCs) cluster heavily in Pune and hire for product engineering at 2–4x IT-services pay. Reorienting from "IT-services fresher" to "GCC engineer" is the single highest-leverage decision — which is why Pune (cheaper living + densest GCC/auto/semiconductor ecosystem) is the recommended location.

4   How the CAP Round Blueprint Was Built (Task 4)

Document checkpoints — reasoning

The plan front-loads NCL and Income certificate renewal because both are annual and both gate the two money tools (TFWS + MahaDBT). An expired NCL silently collapses caste benefit and TFWS to open category — the most common, most avoidable cause of losing a seat/quota.

Choice interleaving — reasoning

CAP allots the first code in the preference list that the candidate is eligible for and that has a vacancy. Therefore ordering = priority, and placing the free TFWS ('T') code directly above the paid regular code guarantees the system tries "free" before "paid" for the same college-branch.

Freeze vs Not-Freeze — reasoning

NOT-FREEZE (Betterment) secures the current seat as a floor while keeping the candidate eligible for an upgrade in the next round; the engine can only move a candidate up their own list, never down. This makes Betterment a risk-free way to keep a financial safety net while reaching for better placements — hence the rule to Not-Freeze in Rounds 1–2 and Freeze only on a clearly best seat.

Confirm the labels: exact option names ("Freeze / Not-Freeze / Float / Auto-freeze") and round mechanics can change year-to-year. The principle (secure floor, climb upward) holds; the button names must be read from the current-year rulebook.
